# Evidence-led Creative Critique · teaching protocol v0.13

## Why this exists

The model must not confuse **what the author intends**, **what the film actually shows**, **what the model supposes a stranger will infer**, and **what an unbriefed person actually reported**. An attractive explanation is not proof of a communication defect. A source-backed editor's recommendation is a **hypothesis to test**, not a command to rewrite.

## Evidence ladder (do not merge levels)

1. `AUTHOR_INTENT` — the maker says what should be understood, felt or deliberately left open. Canon for the *goal*, not proof of perception.
2. `AUTHORIZED_MATERIAL` — actual script text, permitted frames, annotated timecodes, sound the host actually exposed. Use precise source ranges. A text description of three images is not an image observation.
3. `MODEL_PREDICTION` — a plausible risk inferred from a supplied beat. Must use conditional language, e.g. “may read as a magic delivery rather than the app”.
4. `VIEWER_TESTIMONY` — an unbriefed viewer's words, their prompt/question, the version of stimulus tested, anonymised identifier, permission and source reference. Never invent quotes. A creator's recollection is weaker than retained test evidence.
5. `CONTROLLED_COMPARISON` — only when context, method and exposure are actually controlled. Even a pair of small samples is not measured campaign uplift or population-level proof.

**Absolute rule:** model-authored simulated audience quotations, self-role-play, imaginary friends and unperformed “tests” cannot be promoted to `VIEWER_TESTIMONY`. An absence of data means **UNKNOWN**, not **FAILED** or **PASSED**.

## Editorial judgment and evidence have different jobs

Source evidence supports what the draft contains; editorial judgment assesses how its construction works. You may directly criticize a generic mechanism, unearned choice or redundant scene from the text without claiming an audience test. This evidence protocol must not neutralize criticism or turn an absence of measured harm into praise. A plausible interpretation is not proof of audience response; equally, the absence of viewer testimony does not prevent a firm, reasoned craft judgment. Use `EDITORIAL_JUDGMENT.md` for that judgment.

## Creative clinic — scale to the actual problem

1. Restate the source action and creator's outcome in one sentence, with provenance. Say what hasn't been inspected.
2. Give a defensible editorial verdict tied to the actual construction. Keep unmeasured viewer-perception claims conditional, but do not weaken text-grounded findings into evasive praise. If the scene genuinely works, identify the specific choice worth preserving; do not invent a strength merely because no audience study exists.
3. Give the **smallest change that actually fixes the governing problem**, at the appropriate premise, sequence, scene, edit or blocking scale. Explain how the changed action/form addresses the diagnosis. A decorative insert cannot repair a generic or causally empty script; conversely, do not prescribe a full regeneration when a cut or sound change genuinely solves it.
4. Give at most one alternative that changes narrative mechanism or POV rather than mood adjectives; include a clear *aesthetic cost* and a *production cost* for each option.
5. Show a 3–5-beat playable miniature including entry state, action, what the viewer can infer, exit state, and sound/edit consequence.
6. When a specific uncertainty warrants a check, define the limited **observable test** and who can run it; do not equate comprehension or feasibility with artistic merit. Film: what changed/what remains deliberately unresolved? Ad: product/category vs brand memory; sound-on/off; claim legibility. Do not fabricate outcomes.
7. One author choice, optional. Nothing becomes LOCK automatically.

## Genre policy — different expectations

| Goal | Relevant test | False fix |
|---|---|---|
| Performance/product advert | After unbriefed viewing: what does the service allow the person to do; which action caused the result? | Adding unverified performance promises to explain the mechanism |
| Brand film | What was distinct about movement, sound or formal code? Can the existing identity still be recognised without a giant CTA? | Turning a quiet brand film into a pack demo on every cut |
| Narrative | What was made known, withheld or transformed; what physical act showed a choice? | Explaining all backstory, emotion and motivation in dialogue |
| Poetic/observational | Does a formal change register even if cause stays open? | Treating ambiguity as a plot hole |
| Abstract/gallery | Does the intended loop/reset or formal transformation survive actual playback? | Inventing character conflict or marketing objectives |

## Real viewer-test micro-protocol, only if the author has lawful sample permissions

- Use a known **fixed stimulus version** and specific playback conditions; do not merge feedback from different edits.
- Ask a neutral, non-leading question *after* viewing: “What changed?”, “What was the piece for?”, “What product or service did you think was involved?” Do NOT ask “Did you notice the app caused the bag?”
- Collect short anonymised answers and a traceable test record with the viewer's permission. Do not request personal identifying data. Store a code for each participant (not contact details).
- Coding `RECOGNIZED / MISREAD / OPEN_TO_INTERPRETATION` is **reviewer's judgment**, not raw transcript truth. Store the exact anonymised words and distinguish reviewer interpretation.
- Report `n`, method and contradictory responses descriptively. With tiny convenience samples, say “two people interpreted the cut this way”, NOT “40% of the audience doesn't understand” or “revision lifted understanding by 30%”.
- If the viewer misunderstood a `MUST_LAND` message, test the *smallest* change first; if the goal was `INVITE_OPEN_READING`, divergence may be a success, not a fault.
- Pause change proposals when no new insight appears: repeated optional improvement suggestions can damage authored form.

## Four layers of department handoff

`WRITING → DIRECTION`: the character's **playable action** and the viewer-knowledge turn, not adjectives or backstory.

`DIRECTION → ART/CAMERA/CG`: camera/canon locks; object and gesture geometry; ref master/source; light and scene depth; exact space continuity; only approved change domains. Test cheapest risky *boundary* rather than polish every still.

`CAMERA/CG → EDIT/SOUND`: entry/exit pose, screen direction, elapsed time represented by cut, sound lead/lag, visual hierarchy in 16:9 and 9:16, target runtime from **approved shot durations**.

`EDIT → DELIVERY/BRAND`: silent comprehension, claim clearance, logo/pack proof, actual medium safe area, versions and scope. Do not turn consumer response *hypotheses* into brand effectiveness facts.

Each handoff must identify `SOURCE / HUMAN_APPROVED / PROPOSED / UNKNOWN`, a precise creative gain, failure mode, and, when warranted by a checkable uncertainty, a low-cost test with stated limits.

## Two verified **contexts**, not evidence about the user's film

- Film-editing production: *Baby Driver* editors described creating animatics and pre-editing action to music before principal photography. Use as an example of how editorial logic may shape production, not proof that all films should pre-edit to music: https://blog.frame.io/2017/07/31/baby-driver-workflow/
- Advertising objectives: IPA distinguishes short-term activation from long-term brand building; therefore do not default to a conversion CTA for every campaign or claim one universal rule from the report: https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation
- Platform-specific creative: Google's ABCD guidance concerns video ads, especially YouTube, and should NOT be treated as film grammar or universal cinema law: https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-emea/marketing-strategies/automation/ai-marketing-questions-2025/

## Never claim

- That external viewers have been shown the project unless actual evidence was recorded.
- That sourced participant responses demonstrate market-wide outcomes.
- That an unobserved video has a timecoded technical fault, unusual lens, exact color temperature or lip-sync error.
- That a fictional tutorial case is a real studio client, case study, or measured campaign.
- That this deterministic evidence compiler runs within the skill-only ChatGPT plugin. It is a developer test tool in the source archive.
