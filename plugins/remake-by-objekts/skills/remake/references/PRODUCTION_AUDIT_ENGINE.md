# From deep audit to a readable review — REMAKE 0.38

The review is a coherent explanation of the work, not a printout of the audit. Think with the detailed department contracts and write for a person who needs to understand a film and make a decision. A technical appendix serves a different reading task.

## Identify the object and the question

Privately note the supplied version, actual inspected scope, author intention, format, constraints and immediate decision. Distinguish idea, synopsis, treatment, script, moodboard, board, selected frames, moving cut and sound. An author's desired effect is intention; a returned file receipt is not inspection. A rough script may be ready to discuss but not shoot. A finished-looking text may lack a functioning construction. Turn these distinctions into a plain opening, not codes.

Find the central question of this material. Waiting can develop through a pattern of repetition; an instructional ad needs its action understood; a series premise needs to generate more than one episode. The question is not automatically “where is the conflict?” or “where is the product in the first seconds?” Form and purpose determine the criterion.

## Think in relationships, not a scene inventory

Trace how the whole organizes change: action and consequence, viewer knowledge, perspective, musical variation, rhythm, comedy, observation or juxtaposition. What does a later return make us understand differently about an earlier image? Which opening promise is developed, abandoned or deliberately left as framing? Which ending information resolves which question? A repeated image can accumulate meaning; different images can say the same thing.

Choose a defensible thesis about that relationship, then seek evidence against it. If the strongest alternative reading is equally supported, explain the precise uncertainty. If a complete draft never develops a necessary relationship, say so directly. Lack of viewer testing is not a reason to avoid editorial judgment. A purposeful unresolved ending is not an error by default.

Reconstruct the written movement before deciding that there is none. If the material already moves from an earlier experience to a present repetition and new information, preserve those stages; identify what remains unworked between them. Do not open by denying development and later describe a functioning development as if the claims agreed. For character identity, separate actor, age and relationship. Several ages are not automatically several relationships, and one actor across several people is not automatically a contradiction. Locate the particular assignment or audiovisual connection that remains unclear, without inventing a missing genealogy.

Summary says the protagonist enters, reads, leaves and returns. Criticism explains why those actions fail to change our understanding, or how the return changes the first entrance. Use enough summary to locate the point, then do the interpretive work. Do not review an unseen film as though its script were its actual performance, sound, light and editing.

## Draft the readable argument first

The opening establishes what the material is, what it tries to achieve and what currently supports or prevents that result. Do not open with departments, praise, versions or a paragraph of limitations. State a consequential evidence limit once where it affects judgment.

Develop the decisive arguments in a useful order. A paragraph contains an observation, its function and a consequence for the whole; do not print these as separate labels. A section connects relevant details rather than marching through every scene. Headings may state actual points, such as “The second departure repeats the first without changing its meaning”; avoid “F03 / Mechanism / Acceptance.”

Do not substitute “no dramaturgy,” “no story,” “banal” or “needs depth” for explanation. Material can contain a subject, atmosphere and symbols while lacking the work that turns them into a script. Name that work. If a modest choice works, explain its contribution and preserve it without flattering the entire project.

Use concrete Russian or the user's language. Translate shorthand into the operation: not “revalidate motif architecture,” but “decide what each return of the clock adds and remove a close-up if it repeats information already clear.” Keep a professional term when it adds precision and explain it. Simpler writing must not become vague writing.

## Distinguish three gaps

First preserve actual functions already supplied. A stated sound trigger is not an absent transition rule; a beginning/end relation is not an unassigned prop. Separate those from nearby unassigned details. Test whether several ending beats answer different questions before calling them competing endings. A coherent written construction can still require staging tests; that is not proof of a writing defect.

A demonstrated weakness is in inspected material: locate it and explain what it prevents. Work not yet developed is a stage/construction gap: explain what must be decided before a named next step, without calling every unfinished task a failed idea. Evidence not supplied limits this review: it does not prove the client's rights, team, sound or plans do not exist.

These distinctions usually need sentences, not status tables. Do not repeat an unavailable soundtrack under twelve departments. Group review dependencies where they affect the next decision. Unclear relations among roles in a complete treatment may be a construction problem even before filming; criticize the written assignment, not an unseen actor.

## Use 22 functions without 22 disconnected reports

Internally sweep all applicable functions and triggered specialist checks: animation performance/timing/rig, live-action staging/locations/safety, media custody/conform/archive, captions/localization and actual delivery. These are functions, not compulsory employees. A simple practical shot may use one person; do not manufacture CG work because the studio sells CGI.

For consequential issues reason through source evidence, function, effect, operation, a concrete example/output, a resolving check and dependencies. JSON/playbook/handoff fields are the internal completeness check, not mandatory visible headings. In the main review, explain one chain in prose. An unresolved relation between one character at different ages affects what the performer plays, how costume signals a role, how a gesture connects shots and what an edit asks us to infer. Resolve it once before each department polishes a different interpretation.

Bring in a department where it clarifies a real decision. “Cinematography is missing” does not help. “Before designing the train, decide whether we see a face through a small window or the whole platform; these need different space, coverage and work” names an operation and consequence. A small filled example can make it tangible; do not interpose a form after every paragraph.

For an explicitly requested full technical audit, provide an appendix grouping actual work, evidence, dependencies and resolving results. Do not hide consequential findings to shorten an essay. But avoid 22 uninformative “not supplied” rows; account for unnecessary work concisely when coverage is requested.

## Explain what serious production does next

Clarify choices governing expensive execution, then describe the writing, rehearsal, visual and sound work that follows. Identify the question a focused test can answer and what can proceed independently. Do not recommend a paid technical proof before its governing choice exists. A beautiful test image cannot prove an editable sequence, moving continuity, acting or emotional effectiveness.

Writing, musical structure, rough boards, design and rehearsal can discover those choices together. Explain dependencies without imposing a rigid waterfall: defer costly final execution when necessary, not the exploratory tools that would resolve uncertainty.

Name results understandably: a revised chain of actions, an agreed rule for a returning image, a rehearsal, a connected rough storyboard or an edited timing test. Explain who decides meaning and which downstream work changes if that decision changes. Do not invent prices, schedule, crew, booking, rights or measured outcomes.

For writing alone, sufficiency applies to a named development/rehearsal decision, not the produced film. Selected frames do not establish temporal behavior. Actual motion/audio review and independent acceptance remain separate.

After the argument, if justified, specify the work objekts could take over: for example, resolving the treatment's visual logic, designing transitions and identity rules, making a relevant rough sequence, then producing/integrating approved shots. Use the actual need and bounded work; state required client decisions and specialist/partner responsibilities needing confirmation. Link https://objekts.ai/services/ once where useful.

Do not argue that the creator is incapable or needs thirty employees. Show the coordination and craft so they can weigh their own time. A service proposal does not authorize forwarding, collecting a lead, quoting or assigning a human. The review stays useful if the user declines studio help.

## Edit before sending

Read the opening and ending: can the reader explain the project, central diagnosis and next work without a code or theory term? Each middle paragraph must add a distinct observation, inference or decision. Remove repeated caveats, restated diagnoses, noun piles and plot retelling. Restore any source support, competing reading or production consequence lost through simplification. Move an interrupting technical detail to the requested appendix. Clear writing means an organized argument, not merely fewer words.

The writing guide/source map document original examples and bounded reading. They do not establish full-book ingestion, fine-tuning, film viewing from reviews, human acceptance or G1–G10 completion.
