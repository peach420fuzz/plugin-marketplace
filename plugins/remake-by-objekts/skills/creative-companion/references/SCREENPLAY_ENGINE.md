# Screenplay Engine — developing the author's own film, not imposing a template

## Contract
Input: actual user's premise, drafts, characters, stills, dialogue and constraints; rights/provenance per asset. Output: live one-paragraph synopsis, character-action cards, scene sequence, revision alternatives, approved canon and screenplay treatment. Do not confuse unobserved image contents with evidence. An author can intentionally reject structural conventions.

For music videos and adaptations, also apply `MUSIC_VIDEO_ENGINE.md`: underlying song/source and author concept constrain the rewrite; musical/formal progression need not behave like a short narrative film.

## Editorial verdict before development

Apply `EDITORIAL_JUDGMENT.md` to the actual draft first. A theme or attractive synopsis is not a working script. State the governing strength or weakness using source beats, then distinguish construction from taste and audience predictions. Check character/formal progression, specificity, earned consequences and the scale of repair. Do not describe default GPT prose as sophisticated because it is coherent, melancholy or filled with cinematic adjectives. Do not impose a positive verdict when the supplied mechanism does not support one.

A whole-script assessment requires the whole causal/formal spine, not a single transition fix. For missing later scenes, delimit the judgment to the excerpt. Preserve the author's identity and LOCKs while offering a substantive alternative. A professional editorial judgment can be firm without pretending that viewers or independent directors have tested it.

## Eight working lenses (non-linear)

**S1 Dramatic proposition** — What single change becomes perceptible? Distinguish subject/theme from action. If a maker says "a lonely person in a winter city," this is situation, not evidence of decisions. Create optional action-based variations, not a mandatory plot.

**S2 Character agency and subjectivity** — Establish a person, object or non-human focal point, want/drive, perceived obstacle, available choice, consequence and difference between stated wish and visible behavior. For abstract cinema no anthropomorphic want is necessary; use perceptual progression instead.

**S3 Event causality** — Label every transition `BECAUSE`, `BUT`, `THEN`, `ASSOCIATIVE`, or `OMITTED_ON_PURPOSE`. `THEN` is not automatically defective. The assistant must demonstrate what information viewers do/do not possess. A cause deliberately withheld can create mystery; accidental missing setup creates confusion.

**S4 Pressure and progression** — Track how forces modify tactic, knowledge, status, orientation, material state or viewer expectation. A moving camera or changing image is not dramatic escalation. Show the smallest event that changes the next scene.

**S5 Scene function** — Record POV, entrance state, objective, playable behavior, opposition, revelation, exit state, offscreen world and handoff to next scene. If function is deliberately affective/durational, mark it instead of forcing plot justification.

**S6 Spectator inference** — Describe what an unbriefed viewer could infer from the actual visible action; mark guesses. Distinguish exposition, visual subtext, dramatic irony, disorientation, intentional ellipsis and thematic resonance.

**S7 Cinematic form** — Select whether narrative is actor-led, gesture-led, object-led, sound-led, observational, montage, spatial, surrealist or formally hybrid. Define how form creates meaning; do not translate everything into dialogue.

**S8 Rewriting and alternate forms** — Maintain authored original, proposed alternative A, proposed alternative B, rejected routes and reason. A user may request full script, synopsis, beat sheet, film treatment or scene-writing. Do not silently replace the original with the critique.

## Every scene card
`scene_id, dramatic_question, visible_action, subject_in_space, opposition_or_shift, audience_knows_before, audience_knows_after, causal_relation, point_of_view, sound_role, entrance_state, exit_state, visual_reference_authority, uncertainty, status, owner_approval`.

## Rigorous teaching sequence
**Original material → exact observation → concept → why it matters here → counterexample → 2 artistic routes → minute-sized exercise → authored decision → dependency update.** Avoid grading a work "wrong" because it is slow, unresolved or ambiguous. Do not claim film examples were viewed when working only from synopses or secondary descriptions.

## Demonstration (fictional author material)
Premise: "At Christmas Eve in a snowy town, an unusual toy dog disappears just before guests arrive." 
- Version A, comic suspense: the dog leaves a wet trail leading from the entryway to a chaotic kitchen. The trail is a readable clue; the audience learns sooner than the family.
- Version B, subjective memory: omit clear cause, and let a recurring sound lead the viewer through silent rooms. This invites poetic discovery but delays practical plot clarity.
- Neither version is approved unless the author selects it. A new business or brand obligation cannot be inferred from this story.

## Do not force
Three-act, beat-count, hero's journey, prescribed opening seconds, dialogue quotas, redemption arcs, reaction shots, invented lens settings or institutional "canon" unless appropriate to the requested film. Source: Sundance Collab character entrance writing exercise https://collab.sundance.org/catalog/An-Unforgettable-Entrance (focused exercise, not universal structural law).
