# Creative workshop F — a lost toy and a winter tram, 24-second children's animation

**Original invented tutorial; not any existing objekts project.** A fictional winter city called Northhaven, a child named Milo and a wind-up fox named Fern. No claim about an actual town, IP, film, client or observed image. This is **story-to-board**, not character prompt generation.

## Creator's draft

> “A boy and his clockwork toy wait for the last winter tram. The toy falls through a gap, then there's a scene of the boy looking sad, then the toy is back. My first images are charming, but the short film feels flat. I don't want extra dialogue. I want the boy to *do* something.”

**Diagnosis tied to source:** described beats imply the toy returns without a visible *choice*, so the author's intended active character may not yet be legible. Not proof that real footage is flat. A toy's fall is a clear external event, but what matters is the child's response. Propose one physical problem he can solve without adding explanatory voiceover.

**Two distinct options:**

- **A — spatial rescue:** he lies down at the tram stop and uses his scarf to reach the toy safely *without entering the tracks*. Viewer sees a concrete, playful choice; requires geometry and a believable retrieve action. Avoid illustrating unsafe rail behavior as a recommended action; locate the toy in a safe accessible platform recess. An adult/safety supervisor is present but can stay offscreen. The geography must establish that the gap is a non-track platform recess.
- **B — toy responds first:** he turns the toy's crank on a loose winding key while holding it from the safe platform edge; the toy's tiny light provides a visual return signal. More wonder, weaker independent child problem-solving; requires continuity and sound of the winding mechanism, not a physical recovery near moving tram.

**Chosen for sample exercise:** fictional author approves B but asks that the toy stay physically reachable on the platform: NO TRACK REACH. That does not approve any other scene.

## Micro-sequence — 24 seconds (all times are proposals)

| Shot | Time | What the image must establish | Performance and sound | Department consequence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| S01 | 0–5s | Empty tram platform, last tram still far away, toy near bench; boy checks that it is there | No dialogue. Scarf gust and distant bell | Establish the toy's position, platform geography and distance to tracks |
| S02 | 5–10s | A gust pushes the toy into a **shallow recess under the bench**, never onto rail | Child reaches toward it but cannot grasp; small mechanical tick persists | Costume and toy scale, recess geometry, physical staging |
| S03 | 10–16s | Boy spots winding key on scarf and tries it near the recess | Playable action: choose, untie, wind; quiet before a soft click | Insert with hand and key; maintain toy orientation and character hands |
| S04 | 16–20s | Toy light activates, revealing its position. Child reaches safely and retrieves it | Light as reveal, not miracle teleport; sound-on/silent comprehension needs separate test | Lighting and occlusion; eye-line; visual effect continuity |
| S05 | 20–24s | Child boards with toy as last tram arrives. No speech or emotive close-up needed | Bell repeats as a narrative rhyme | Exit direction, angle and sound-bridge to ending |

**Why the original felt flat (hypothesis):** “falls → looks sad → returns” outsources the change to plot convenience; “falls → tries → changes what becomes visible → retrieves” makes the child own a decision. The new idea must be tested for comprehensibility, and may be rejected if the maker actually wants pure mood rather than agency.

## Department teaching & acceptance

- **Script:** the active verb is *wind*, not “feels hope”. The obstacle changes what action is possible. Prove an action that a child can perform in the world of the film.
- **Direction:** hold one geometry across S02/S03 so the toy doesn't teleport. Put the key exactly where the child can discover it. Actor's gaze leads insert placement.
- **Art and character:** lock the fox shape, winding key orientation, winter glove styling and toy scale from approved master. Do not assume how an unseen render depicts them.
- **Lighting:** toy light changes *information* in S04; that is its story purpose, not merely atmosphere.
- **Edit:** the wait at S03 earns S04; cut only when audience knows why light turning on matters. A short animatic with drawings is a cheaper proof than completing CGI.
- **Sound:** the tiny mechanical tick bridges S02→S03, bell brackets beginning/end. Silent version must remain legible if required for feed.
- **Production/child-safety:** fiction must not suggest reaching live tracks. Show safe platform recess, distant tram, supervised action if performed physically.
- **QC:** check full sequence before grading, not five isolated picturesque frames. Exact toy position, gaze, hand/key contact and background geometry are continuity anchors.

## Counterfactual learning exercise

Make two 12-second animatics from the same approved art: one where the toy returns on its own, another where the winding action reveals and enables recovery. Show each silently to people unfamiliar with the pitch and ask **“What did the child do that changed the outcome?”** Record actual answers before saying a version 'works'. Do not present expected replies as measurements. Ask the author which version belongs to their story; do not force a CGI budget or a studio inquiry.
