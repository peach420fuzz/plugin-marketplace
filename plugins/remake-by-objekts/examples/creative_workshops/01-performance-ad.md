# Creative workshop A — 15-second delivery-app film

**Fictional editorial case, not an observed user or verified campaign.** The imaginary product is a grocery delivery application called **HereNow**. The brand name, slogan and features are invented only for this educational case. We are NOT claiming delivery time, speed, nutrition or any market position. Author intent is product comprehension and click-through; actual CTA and claims require a real brief.

## Creator's existing draft

> "A tired architect looks into an empty fridge. Suddenly a bag with groceries materialises on the counter. The kitchen becomes a colourful city. Tagline: 'Life happens.' I have one nice kitchen image and two city shots. Fifteen seconds vertical."

### Assistant, turn 1 — locate one real creative hinge

**What is provided:** empty fridge, inexplicable bag, city transformation, unspecific phrase. Images are described, not inspected. **What the viewer knows:** some kind of magic has happened; neither the app nor the choice the protagonist makes is visible. The experience may be memorable, but it would communicate almost any lifestyle brand.

**Route A — decision-driven commercial:** the architect pushes aside a spread of blueprints, sees the empty fridge, makes one visible selection in the app, and at the instant the interface closes a *match cut* takes us to the filled counter. The audience sees whose agency caused the change. Cost: interface graphics must be accurate and readable without relying on AI-generated text. The magic becomes less mysterious.

**Route B — experience-led brand film:** use the sound of a notification as an anticipatory bridge, let the filled counter appear before revealing the app in the protagonist's hand in a single controlled composition. Keeps wonder; viewers could misattribute the transformation until the reveal, so the pack/app icon must be distinctly designed and legible.

**Mini exercise:** mute the soundtrack. In one sentence, what will a viewer say is being advertised after shot 3? If the answer is "a magical kitchen", add a visual cue before polishing effects. Neither route is approved yet. **Question:** is the most important outcome for this placement app comprehension or a branded mood?

### Imaginary author decision

> "They must understand it is an app. Route A. Don't turn it into a fantasy-ad campaign."

**Approved direction:** visible choice in app. **LOCK:** grounded reality / no magical city. The original city transformation becomes a **rejected branch**, not forgotten inspiration waiting to reappear.

### Assistant, turn 2 — teach visual causality with a beat map

| Proposed beat | Visual action | Viewer inference | New craft requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Protagonist closes an empty refrigerator, glances to a late-night drawing desk | She has a task and lacks ingredients | Environment + performance must identify the situation without explanatory caption |
| 2 | On her phone she picks items in an app | There is an app and she chooses | UI graphics built as controlled layers, not text generated in video |
| 3 | Match cut from phone slipping onto desk to bag being set onto the desk by someone offscreen | The selected order arrived; time has elapsed | This is an edit, NOT a claim of instant delivery |
| 4 | She resumes work; small reveal of recognisable app identity | Product role and resolution | Need a real brand/UI master before claiming 'production ready' |

**Director's note:** the protagonist is not "impressed"; she makes a practical choice and returns to the drawing. A silent nod can underplay the convenience. A magical city is no longer permitted. **Editing caution:** the match cut signifies time elision, not impossibly fast service; if ad copy claims a delivery guarantee that would require independent proof.

### Imaginary author decision

> "I want the offscreen hand setting the bag down. No faces besides the architect. Keep 9:16 composition."

**Approved:** one lead subject; delivered bag enters from offscreen; vertical intent. **Still unknown:** real legal UI screens, brand icon, channel CTA and app features. The pack cannot be final with fake icon/claim.

### Assistant, turn 3 — director's shot function rather than pretty stills

- **S01:** narrow vertical kitchen geography. Entrance fridge open → action door closes → exit gaze toward desk. This eye-line tells editor where cut S02 must land.
- **S02:** controlled phone comp. Entrance screen stable → fingers make selection → exit phone lowers. Exact action should be shown at appropriate duration; do not fabricate 'two taps' unless UI supports them.
- **S03:** desk anchor and bag arrival; enter on screen direction inherited from S02. Audio cue can bridge an intentionally unspecified elapsed time. Test whether the hand looks like an actual delivery rather than appearing out of nowhere.
- **S04:** character returns to work as app/logo cue is clear. The ending is a useful outcome, not a drawn-out superhero reveal.

**Test before video generation:** paper animatic with placeholder UI, silent and sound-on versions. Ask unbriefed volunteers to identify (a) category, (b) action that caused arrival, (c) what is a real promise vs editorial ellipsis. Count responses honestly; this case records NO results.

**Production-aware handoff:** screenplay and camera/staging → graphics team gets UI master need; storyboard → edit gets gaze and screen-direction continuity; VFX → only needed if synthetic bag/hand must integrate. Author can make it independently. No contact request or pricing.

## Distinctive teaching principle

A commercial does not require more spectacle to become clearer. Here, removing the unreal city and adding a small **on-screen choice** radically changes viewer inference. Distinguish narrative **ellipsis** from unverified product performance.
