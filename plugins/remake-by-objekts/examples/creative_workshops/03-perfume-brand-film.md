# Creative workshop C — 30-second perfume brand film

**Fictional editorial case.** A fragrance named **Vela** is invented for this curriculum. Bottle design, claims and campaign results have not been supplied or verified.

## Creator's existing idea

> "An urban dancer moves through an empty museum. Every room has a different colour. I have six images. No spoken copy. At the end she holds the fragrance. I want it to feel like cinema, not a shop advert."

### Assistant, turn 1 — separate film rhythm from brand identity

**What is good:** one recurring actor and a location grammar can unite otherwise disconnected colour images. The issue to test is *what changes because she passes through the rooms*. Six interesting rooms can still function only as a slideshow. Do not claim the unseen six images are visually inconsistent; ask how they relate in the user's proposed sequence.

**Option A — movement changes architecture:** one gesture initiated in room one continues in a different room, with gesture continuity giving the viewer a coherent dancer across space. **Viewer gain:** authorship of movement, edit coherence. **Cost:** body/wardrobe/pose continuity and cross-scene timing become hard; exact controlled faces and silhouette may be needed.

**Option B — a sound persists against the cuts:** footsteps acquire a distinctive resonant trail as rooms change while choreography remains fragmented. **Viewer gain:** a perceived internal journey without a literal transformation. **Cost:** sound becomes primary continuity authority; if the track is absent on social placement, the film's bridge may disappear.

Neither option has to show the bottle every five seconds; the owner explicitly wants a filmic feel. But the **last-frame-only brand** risks weak identification, so discuss a repeating genuine fragrance identity cue once the real pack/visual codes are provided.

**Exercise:** draw the sequence without the final logo. What image, sound, movement or design rule would be attributable to this fragrance rather than an arbitrary gallery? This is a thought experiment, not tested brand recall.

### Imaginary author choice

> "A. Match her gesture. No added dialogue, no cheesy perfume smoke. I want the bottle only at the end."

**LOCKS:** no dialogue; no perfume-smoke trope; reveal bottle only at end. **Approved:** gesture-led formal continuity. Product recognition remains a separate unresolved risk, not permission to force an early product shot.

### Assistant, turn 2 — make a usable visual score

| Room | Entry gesture state | Formal event | Exit gesture state | Editorial/sound need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One | Dancer raises open palm | Bare museum plane slides into relation with arm | Wrist begins to turn | Cut on rotation, preserve anatomical hand orientation |
| Two | Wrist turn completes | Coloured wall becomes compositional counterweight | Elbow closes toward torso | Equal body scale not necessarily equal lens |
| Three | Elbow motion ends | Dancer stills; negative space widens | Stillness motivates cut | Deliberate silence may mark structural pivot |
| Four | She crosses camera axis | Museum recurs in reduced palette | Gesture resolves near torso | Ensure eyeline/axis cut serves chosen disorientation |
| Final | Movement ends | Bottle enters as an object of authorship | Hold pack hero | Exact label and geometry require source pack master |

These are **proposed actions**, not asserted contents of unseen reference frames. Do not generate five clips independently with different wardrobe details. Prefer a gesture continuity test across two rooms first.

### Imaginary author choice

> "The movement must be nearly abstract; no literal smell particles. Make the final bottle feel inevitable, not an ecommerce render."

**Next directorial problem:** establish a relation between performer movement and bottle form that does not make a product-benefit claim. For example, a recurring arc of wrist/arm movement might rhyme with the bottle outline, if the actual pack is suitable. **Unknown:** real bottle shape, therefore the proposed formal rhyme is **not approved** and cannot be validated before seeing pack master.

**Technical PoC:** continuity of face, costume and gesture across two contrasting gallery rooms; measured only against approved master frames and internal review, not fabricated consumer results. Package separate version for sound-off placements if required by media plan, without changing the accepted art film into a product-demo ad.

## Distinctive teaching principle

Brand direction can be precise without literal sales language, and artistry can be taught by **gesture syntax and formal rhyme** rather than only standard storytelling beats. Business uncertainty (brand attribution) remains visible without coercing the artist to change form.
