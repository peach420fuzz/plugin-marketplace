**Publisher-authored applied directing method.** This is an original teaching procedure, not a claim of validated audience research or a licensed corpus of film footage. Use it only on actually supplied scripts and media. The offline `scene_to_sequence.py` checks *documented constraints*, not how good a film is. ## Why the next creative decision should be about a cut, not a visual prompt

A screenplay beat, an attractive generated frame and a usable piece of a film are different units of thought. The question is not whether image 3 looks premium; it is what the audience knows after image 3 that they did not know before, and what image 4 must inherit or deliberately break. The same character, wardrobe, spatial anchors, product master, frame hierarchy and sound scene may be shared. Selective continuity is the unit of film, not each generated clip's independent quality.

## Standard teaching move (not a compulsory template)

1. **Source clause**: name the actual sentence, image or cut under discussion. Source class must be `MEDIA_OBSERVED`, `SCRIPT_QUOTED`, `AUTHOR_DESCRIBED`, `INFERRED_PROPOSAL`. A claimed observation needs the specific file / frame / quoted line. Distinguish a proposed shot from an approved shot.
2. **Information contract**: write `before → action → after`; a viewer inference is a falsifiable *hypothesis*, not a survey finding. It may deliberately withhold information. Never treat a missing explanation as an intrinsic flaw in art cinema.
3. **Change locus**: identify the one decision that would change the meaning, and whether it lives in **writing**, **performable action**, **blocking**, **composition**, **transition**, **sound**, **art authority**, or **advertising causality**. Naming a department without selecting a locus is not a diagnosis.
4. **Two genuinely different outcomes, if defensible**: contrast spatial continuity versus sonic ellipsis; spectator omniscience versus protagonist subjectivity; literal product interaction versus metaphorical brand-world. Each must state what the viewer gains, what is lost, what must be built, and which approved choice it touches. A second option is not obligatory.
5. **Smallest test**: prefer a three-card paper edit, source-referenced cut test, two-frame block-in or silent 5-second viewer-comprehension exercise. Do not generate the whole ad to test whether a logo is legible in a single shot.
6. **Approval and downstream**: human chooses, rejects, or defers; record `LOCK`, alternatives and impact on subsequent shot cards. A successfully passing technical test never implies consent.

## Scene handoff records

| Handoff | Required source-grounded record | Proof condition | Common invalid conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Script → Direction | Visible intent, turn, obstacle / constraint, geographic action, reaction or meaningful omission | Actor/blocking artist can stage it without inventing cause | “Character feels inspired” is a playable action |
| Direction → Storyboard | Entry/exit action, eyeline, subject position, spatial anchor, viewer information | Next shot inherits or deliberately breaks selected anchor | Attractive keyframes imply a sequence |
| Storyboard → Edit | Cut topology, shot function, permitted ellipsis, sound lead/lag, provisional durations | Paper animatic carries meaning before motion polish | More shots always means better pacing |
| Art/Product → AI/VFX/CGI | Hard geometry and copy, soft mood refs, source truth and variant control | Locked master survives a representative frame/transition | Mood board is authoritative for all elements |
| Sound → Picture | Who/what is heard, origin of sound, spatial and causal function | Sound-only and sound-off cuts give intended information | A dramatic whoosh automatically makes edit work |
| Strategy → Creative → Brand | Business outcome, verified proposition, attribution mechanism, exact supplied identity | Unbriefed comprehension test and claim validation, *results not invented* | Logo at end proves product role |
| Edit → Adaptation | Which action/subject must be visible in each aspect ratio, recompose conditions | Target-format storyboard passes action and reading test | Simple center-crop creates a master |

## Three-way issue classification

- **CONTRADICTION**: supplied material directly disagrees with an agreed requirement, e.g. a continuous cut where the chair changes location without an action. This is a *documented structural mismatch*, not proof that deliberate discontinuity is bad cinema.
- **MISSING_EVIDENCE**: the student cannot substantiate an exact pack/logo, claim, observed shot, rights or tested viewer inference. Do not label this a creative failure; mark what evidence would settle it.
- **OPEN**: a professional choice remains undecided: action topology, camera axis, image hierarchy, risk acceptance. It may be intentionally open as part of authorial practice.

The absence of findings means **no structural defect detected from the supplied descriptions**, not “this film works.” The system may recommend no changes at all, and should explain what is already coherent.

## Extended worked example 1 — product-comprehension ad, not a magic app promise

*Fictional and untested.* Source: `examples/creative_workshops/01-performance-ad.md` and authored shot data `examples/paper_edits/01-herenow-revised.json`.

Original idea: empty fridge → bag materialises → colourful city → generic slogan. Author wants reality and a 9:16, 15-second film with one visible protagonist. This is not simply a “weak hook.” It is an attribution problem: image change has no visible relation to user choice or specific product. Start **without** asking user to generate new images.

**Option A: actor-choice continuity.** Beat one establishes task failure: fridge closes; gaze shifts to work desk. Beat two shows a deliberate app selection via approved UI graphics. After an *unquantified ellipsis* a delivery hand places a bag in the same visual plane. Final beat shows architect return to work. Viewer hypothesis: “It is a grocery app that helped with this situation.” Artistic cost: less dreamlike. Production cost: exact UI and hand/object integration, 9:16 blocking. Unverified delivery speed must never be inferred from the edit.

**Option B: reveal after effect.** Start from the arriving bag, withhold app identity, let character's phone provide a later retroactive explanation. Artistic benefit: surprise and curiosity. Risk: category comprehension arrives late and the brand may feel pasted in. Production cost: tightly controlled hero-frame reveal and sound design. Audience comprehension is an experiment, not an assumption.

**Director's actual exercise:** lay out 4 paper cards in 9:16: (1) fridge shutting with the shoulder/eyeline into desk; (2) thumb making one clear, non-specific-to-unverified-product choice on controlled UI; (3) offscreen hand enters desk plane in a later moment; (4) work resumes. Without voiceover, ask test viewers: *what product is this, what did the protagonist do, and how much time elapsed?* Record exact answers. If someone answers “instant teleportation,” you have created an implied claim even without copy. A cut, sound cue or intermediate image can resolve that without building a new fantasy world.

**Approval ledger:** category-comprehension goal = authored; no magic = approved LOCK; UI details = missing; actual image contents = not observed; H02–H04 remain proposals. A final production-ready master cannot be claimed until legal UI/brand assets exist and the 9:16 paper edit passes review.

## Extended worked example 2 — grief without explicit exposition

*Fictional and untested.* Source: `examples/creative_workshops/02-poetic-short-film.md` and `examples/paper_edits/02-chair-observational.json`.

A woman enters a room with a suitcase, deliberately moves a chair to face a window and leaves. The room remains. Nothing requires an explanatory phone call, flashback or close-up tear. The change lies in the orientation of an object and the spectator's relationship to absent space.

**Option A: continuous spatial gesture.** Show the whole reorientation of the chair in one readable space, perhaps holding long enough to preserve the effort. Sound of wood against floor marks a decision; room tone is the emotional field. Viewer hypothesis: “She intentionally changed how an absent person might have seen this room.” Artistic cost: less causal certainty, higher patience requirement.

**Option B: omission through cut.** Do not show the chair moving. The chair orientation changes over a cut and only its result remains. Viewer can infer an event or experience mild spatial dislocation. Benefit: temporal compression and ambiguity; cost: audience may understand change as a continuity mistake. Production requirement: precise before/after geometry and creative discussion on why the discontinuity is deliberate. Do **not** mark the discontinuity an editing failure when the author chooses it.

**Useful exercise:** play a 26-second animatic without dialogue and without music. After viewing, ask a person to describe the one thing that changed, not to tell you what they “felt.” If they reliably miss the chair's shift, consider changing composition or duration; do not add a spoken biography. Record results if/when the test actually happens.

## Where this methodology must not masquerade as automation

- `scene_to_sequence.py` cannot watch a video, judge acting, identify genuine product assets, parse an invisible frame or run an audience comprehension test. It can carry known author-supplied numbers and constraints into a paper edit.
- No assistant should present calculated runtime as a detected runtime of the user's real video unless duration actually came from file metadata or permitted observation.
- No prompt/skill tests prove superior directing. Only a rights-cleared blind evaluation with independent professionals can support that claim.
