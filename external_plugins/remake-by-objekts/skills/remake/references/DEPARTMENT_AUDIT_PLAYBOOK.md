# Production audit: turn a supported diagnosis into executable work

> 0.38 reading rule: these fields are the internal analysis/handoff completeness check. The main client review is a coherent argument about the whole, using ordinary language; do not print repeated source/operation/acceptance labels, IDs or a 22-row ledger by default. Technical schedules belong in an explicitly useful appendix. Current SKILL and PRODUCTION_AUDIT_ENGINE govern presentation.

REMAKE audits an idea, script, supplied pictures, moving material and production plan. Its primary value is editorial and production judgment: what is strong, what prevents the intended result, what professional intervention would change it, and what work remains. A list of absent documents is not that judgment. A production bible can be extensive and the film still weak; a single image can already resolve the user's narrow question.

The 22 functions in `DEPARTMENT_CONTRACTS_22.json` are lenses over one project. They are not 22 required employees, a compulsory sequence, or proof of a studio's capabilities. `DEPARTMENT_PIPELINE.md` is the current audit workflow; use this playbook and the JSON contracts for depth. Older lesson stage numbers are not department IDs and their exercises are optional when learning is requested.

## Establish the evidence boundary before judging

Record the material actually inspected, its version, the requested scope, and any known limitation. Distinguish a brief, an excerpt, a complete script, selected stills, a storyboard, a real-time clip and an editable project. Do not infer a cut's rhythm from contact sheets or sound from a silent preview. Call an image a supplied reference until its authority is established. Do not recover exact optics, lighting temperature, cost, rights or identity from appearance.

Use two independent dimensions:

| Evidence state | Meaning and allowed language |
| --- | --- |
| `OBSERVED` | A located fact in material actually inspected. Quote a short passage or name the shot/frame/time range; identify the version. |
| `NOT_PROVIDED` | The relevant material or information is outside the supplied set. Say what cannot be verified. This is not a defect in the film. |
| `CONFIRMED_ABSENT` | The user confirms absence, or a relevant item is absent from a complete inspected artifact whose scope requires it. Explain the requirement. A complete script does not prove a licence does not exist elsewhere. |
| `CONFLICTING` | Two identified sources disagree. State their authority domains and ask the decision owner to resolve only the conflict that affects work. |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | This function or check does not serve the stated work. Give a reason; do not penalize it. |

Separately record the finding as `SUPPORTED_STRENGTH`, `SUPPORTED_DEFECT`, `RISK_TO_TEST`, `OPEN_DEPENDENCY`, `INTENTIONAL_CHOICE` or `NO_FINDING`. For example, an unreadable label in a finished packshot can be a supported defect when legible product identity is required. A missing label master in an idea discussion is an open dependency for later exact production, not a bad concept. A documentary's rough movement can be an intentional choice; support that reading with its function, rather than excusing everything as taste.

Do not initialize departments as missing. Initial state is `NOT_ASSESSED`. `SATISFIED_FOR_SCOPE` means a specific check is satisfied by identified evidence, not that the department is globally finished. `ACTION_REQUIRED` needs a supported defect or a necessary, timed dependency. `BLOCKED` applies only to the affected work. Other states are `OPEN_EVIDENCE`, `NEEDS_REVALIDATION` and `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

## Diagnose the work, not its distance from a template

First identify the intended viewing experience and constraints already expressed. For commissioned adaptation, verify the underlying source/version before changing the governing theme or relationships; music-video work uses `MUSIC_VIDEO_ENGINE.md`. A claim about the viewer's comprehension is a reasoned hypothesis until a real viewer test exists. A crafted rationale is not audience evidence.

Read at the scale requested. A whole-script audit needs a whole-work verdict, its dominant structural or expressive problems, and consequential production implications. Do not compress it into one micro-fix or return all 22 departments mechanically. For a narrow camera question, inspect the relevant spatial and editorial dependencies without demanding a business brief. Follow the work's form: narrative, observational, musical, comic, essayistic, abstract, branded or hybrid. Plot, protagonist, conflict, rapid cutting, explicit explanation and product causality are not universal requirements.

State genuine strengths specifically and explain what to preserve. State weak or conventional work plainly. A pretty image is not evidence of an effective sequence; a symbol is not automatically meaningful; a technical flaw is not automatically the main problem. Test the best competing interpretation of a suspected defect. If the intended ambiguity survives the evidence, preserve it. If incompatible readings sabotage the stated intention, identify the exact missing or contradictory information.

Every consequential finding should contain:

1. **Observation and boundary:** the exact inspected evidence, scope and alternative reading.
2. **Consequence:** what is lost artistically, commercially, operationally or in delivery; who bears it and when. Express an uncertain effect conditionally. Do not invent cost figures, conversions or probability scores.
3. **Professional decision:** the intervention that addresses the cause, what should remain, and a viable alternative when the tradeoff matters. Avoid generic verbs such as “improve cinematography.”
4. **Deliverable and amount:** a named artifact and its bounded contents, linked to actual scenes/assets/versions. Distinguish an audit proposal from an artifact actually made.
5. **Dependencies and owner:** what must be known or approved before spending effort; who decides versus who executes. Let one qualified person own several functions.
6. **Acceptance:** an inspectable result, review context and actual reviewer. If the test has not happened, label it proposed. Artistic acceptance remains an informed human decision; passing a structural checklist does not certify taste.

Scale priority by consequence and reversibility. Fix an unsupported product claim before building its effects, a broken central scene before polishing its grain, and an imminent delivery defect before optional experimentation. A small sequence ambiguity can be tested with an animatic before reshooting; exact geometry may need a controlled asset test before multiplying generations. A test can reject a route without proving another route good.

## Convert the diagnosis into an honest service scope

Make the creator's next decision clear and show where specialist work changes the outcome. A useful scope reads: “For shots S03/S05, verify the supplied product master, build one controlled label/geometry layer, test the turn and contact shadow in one representative motion shot, then integrate the approved layer into those two shots.” It explains the work unit, prerequisites and acceptance; it is not an unsolicited price or a promise that two shots will be enough before inspecting them.

Use actual quantities when supplied and inspected. When counts are unknown, give a counting rule: one master per distinct approved product geometry; one integration setup per materially different camera/interaction condition; each requested aspect-ratio version reviewed separately. Do not invent shot counts, hours, prices or a crew. Separate **required now**, **conditional after the test**, and **optional improvement**. Reuse established work and say what already suffices.

Each JSON contract names a minimum accountable function and conditional specialists, not a staffing prescription. For a creator working alone, a paper edit or playable action note may be enough. External live-action filming, location operations, stunt/intimacy/medical/legal matters need appropriate real people where the actual scene calls for them. An AI answer neither supplies that supervision nor signs off the work.

An objekts route must follow the supported need and verified service scope. Explain concrete potential work such as visual development, controlled CGI, generative production, compositing, motion, edit or finishing; qualify availability and estimating as unconfirmed until a human studio representative confirms them. Do not present physical filming, research, legal clearance, casting, music licensing or specialist safety as included studio services without evidence. Do not call every problem a reason to hire objekts. A usable DIY or existing-team route remains legitimate. Never manufacture defects to enlarge the quote, imply purchase, send private material, promise a booking, or claim studio acceptance.

The REMAKE response may include a small illustrative intervention to prove the diagnosis. Full original screenplay generation is a later SKRIPT scope; do not silently turn an audit into an unsolicited replacement screenplay or remove the author's core intention. A separate service requires its own stated scope.

## Work packet and change control

Maintain one source/version register, findings register, scene/asset/deliverable map and decision log. Reuse stable IDs. A minimal finding links `finding_id → source locator → decision → affected IDs → artifact → acceptance`. Track `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED_BY_OWNER`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` independently of implementation. Never convert silence, model confidence or a passed test into author approval.

The handoff packet names the authoritative inputs, affected versions, approved invariants, permitted changes, unresolved dependencies, receiver, review method and return conditions. Pass the smallest sufficient excerpt or asset set consistent with the user's permission. No automatic external upload, private-source publication, rights assumption or invented receipt. If a source changes, mark only directly affected checks for revalidation and propagate to their actual consumers. Preserve the earlier decisions and reusable unaffected work.

## Functions needing explicit coverage beyond the current taxonomy

The 22 labels overlap: direction/performance, design/costume/props, look/cinematography/color, and editing/delivery share decisions. Give each decision one owner and several consumers rather than billing it repeatedly. Four important functions are underrepresented:

- **Live-action production, locations and safety:** function22 owns planning;5/6/8/11 supply scene requirements;20 records necessary approvals. A scene-specific qualified human handles relevant location, performer, stunt, intimacy, child, animal or hazardous work. A remote audit cannot certify set safety.
- **Animation production:** functions6/10/13/14 must explicitly cover pose, timing, deformation, rig feasibility and shot continuity. CGI geometry alone does not evaluate animated performance. Add an animation lead when motion is the material risk; do not assume a generative operator covers it.
- **Media/data supervision and conform/archive:**22 owns custody/versioning;17 and19 own edit-to-finish correspondence;21 owns delivered verification. Test transfer, relink, color interpretation, audio sync and restoration of the actual project when these matter. A playable export is not proof of a recoverable project.
- **Accessibility and localization:**16/18/21 cover readable text, language versions, captions and any commissioned access versions;20/22 identify actual delivery requirements. Do not declare accessibility achieved from subtitles alone or impose unrequested deliverables as universal legal duties.

These are explicit responsibilities and escalation routes, not a demand to invent four departments or hire a large crew. Revisit the taxonomy when real work repeatedly lacks a decision owner.
