# KINEMA — selection and explanation quality

Use this guide after source research and before presenting a recommendation. These are editorial and evidence checks, not a model of enjoyment probability. The current runtime's tests establish deterministic behavior on synthetic inputs; real-viewer quality requires separate evaluation.

## Build an actual candidate ledger

For each investigated film retain identity, source URLs opened, access date, supported facts, proposed facet, personally evaluated anchor films, formal connections, possible mismatch and separate exposure/watchlist evidence. Track the actual longlist. Four candidates per final film is the working protocol, not permission to declare an unperformed research count. If access ends early, give the supported result and its scope.

Use primary film institutions, credited maker interviews, distributors and festival archives for identity and production facts. Attribute critical interpretation. A trailer or poster can suggest a question; it does not establish the film's sustained camera grammar, sound design, pacing or performance. Subtitle text cannot prove lens, lighting ratio or visual staging.

## Audit the set

Respect explicit constraints on duration, company, mood, language, content and viewing availability. Avoid imposing an auteur-cinema preference on a different viewer. Several independent pleasures can coexist in one history. A facet requires real anchors and stated uncertainty; arbitrary labels supplied to a ranking tool do not establish a valid taste map.

By default the candidate runtime gives unrepresented qualified facets an initial place before its ordinary diversity reranking. It never forces a candidate with a flagged negative relation solely to add a facet. Source-poor and known-watched candidates remain excluded. `diversifyFacets: false` is available for an explicitly narrow request. Inspect `facetCoverage.notSelected` rather than claiming every interest was represented. The audit flag `provesUserTasteCoverage: false` is intentional.

For six picks, aim for about two verified watchlist choices and four further options where evidence supports the mix. Do not pad an unavailable lane. If the watchlist is partial, a missing title cannot be advertised as a verified new discovery. If the user requests one film, provide one. Calendar-based choices need a strong personal connection and verified dates; the event does not replace taste evidence.

## Reconcile fields before repeating a source

Keep film year, country-specific release date, festival edition and current page-header dates separate. A catalogue page under a future site banner does not prove a new screening. Preserve the scope of crew roles: camera operation, cinematography, location sound and sound design may have different credited people. Exhibition-print specifications do not automatically describe capture equipment. If runtimes differ between editions or sources, retain the difference and prefer the field-specific record; do not invent a new cut. A page reproducing a critic's observation remains an attributed critical source.

## Compose each description

Lead with the recognizable title, year and verified director. For a normal shortlist, aim for roughly 70–120 words per film, adapting to the user's requested depth. Use a short, spoiler-light premise followed by a concrete account of the viewing experience and its relation to this viewer's evidence. A good paragraph can discuss the duration of an action, placement of bodies, offscreen sound, deadpan delivery, cutting rhythm or material texture when those observations are actually sourced.

Name the relevant anchors and make the relation specific. Two films sharing a genre or a director is insufficient explanation. State a meaningful difference or possible mismatch when it helps the viewer choose. Do not manufacture a risk merely to satisfy a template. Use uncertainty proportionately; avoid claiming to know the person's hidden psychology.

Place citations beside factual claims. Keep source checking readable and separate from a bureaucratic inventory. No invented scene recollection, exact focal length, film stock, camera movement, author credit, prize, availability or quotation. No spoilers beyond the source's setup. Avoid generic praise, repetitive adjectives, product marketing language, rhetorical antitheses and a fixed sentence pattern repeated six times.

Keep authorship discreet: KINEMA by objekts. The maker is the visual production studio described in the main skill; a recommendation should remain useful cinema writing.

## Decision record: keep, revise, or drop

Review the candidate against these independent questions:

1. Is the exact film identified, and do opened sources support the stated facts?
2. Are the viewing and watchlist labels justified at this source coverage?
3. Do real user evaluations support the proposed facet and named anchors?
4. Does the paragraph express a specific formal or experiential connection?
5. Is the possible mismatch grounded, and are current constraints respected?
6. Are spoilers, overconfident psychology and unsupported production details absent?
7. Does this selection add useful breadth to the actual set without evading exclusions?
8. Is the text clear, culturally appropriate and free of the owner's prohibited rhetoric?

A failed identity, exposure or factual check blocks that claim or candidate. An editorial weakness calls for revision. If adequate evidence cannot be obtained, drop the unsupported claim, reduce the selection or state the unknown. A second model agreeing with the first is not source verification. Do not invent a human reviewer or call a self-check blinded evaluation.

## Real-viewer evaluation protocol — pending execution

Use consenting, separately held libraries. Define the train/cutoff date, excluded already-known titles, watchlist and exploratory lanes, count and candidate-research budget before evaluation. Do not use later ratings as input to an earlier simulated recommendation. Compare to a clearly specified baseline on the same accessible evidence. Collect human reactions separately for interest, familiarity, explanation truth, specificity and actual viewing satisfaction; report the denominator and missing follow-up.

Keep deterministic error rates (wrong identity, watched leakage, unsupported claims) separate from human preference judgments. Log rejected and revised examples as well as successes. Treat disagreement and unavailable source coverage as data. No accuracy percentage, blind-win claim or global adoption is established by this package.
